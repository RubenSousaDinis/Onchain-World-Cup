## Post 1 — Publish April 4 (Today)
**Title:** Introducing Onchain World Cup

**Subtitle:** The World Cup, decided onchain. No FIFA. No matches. Just community.

---

We're building a new kind of World Cup.

Not a prediction market. Not a fantasy game. Not a tournament simulator. Something simpler and more interesting: a community vote, on Base, where fan support decides everything.

Here's how it works.

**211 nations. 48 spots.**

Every eligible national team starts on an equal footing. There are no seeds, no rankings, no automatic qualifiers. The only thing that matters is how much support a country can generate from its global fanbase.

Fans vote with ETH. The 48 countries with the most votes qualify for the Onchain World Cup bracket — group stages, knockout rounds, a final. A champion is crowned before the real FIFA World Cup 2026 kicks off on June 11.

**Voting with ETH — and why it matters**

Free voting is broken. It invites bots, spam, and coordinated manipulation. ETH-based voting solves this: each vote costs real money, so support is genuine.

But there's more. The ETH you spend doesn't disappear — 90% of it flows back to supporters of qualifying countries, proportional to how many votes they cast. Voting isn't just participation. It's a stake.

**The early-voter advantage**

Each country has its own independent pricing curve. The first vote for any country costs 0.001 ETH. Each additional vote for that same country increases the price by 0.0005 ETH.

This means the earliest supporters of a country that goes on to qualify earn proportionally more from the prize pool. Conviction pays. Bandwagoning costs more.

It also means that a passionate, organised fanbase that mobilises early can outcompete a much larger but slower-moving one.

**Built on Base**

Gas fees on Base are under $0.01 per transaction. The ETH you spend is almost entirely the vote price itself. You can connect with any Base-compatible wallet, or just sign in with email or Google — no crypto experience required.

All voting logic and prize distribution are handled by open-source smart contracts. Payouts are automatic. There's no middleman, no manual process, no trust required.

**Qualification opens April 14 at 10am CT.**

One week to decide which 48 countries make it. After that, the bracket is set.

## Post 2 — Publish April 9
**Title:** How to Win at Onchain World Cup

**Subtitle:** A strategy guide for the qualification phase.

---

Onchain World Cup isn't pure speculation. The mechanics reward specific behaviours — and understanding them gives you a real edge.

Here's how to think about the qualification phase.

**The goal**

Qualification runs April 14–21. The 48 countries with the most votes advance. Your goal is to hold votes in qualifying countries when the phase ends.

Votes for countries that don't qualify earn nothing. Votes for countries that do qualify earn a share of the unified prize pool — proportional to your vote count relative to all votes cast for qualifying countries.

**Strategy 1: Go early on high-confidence countries**

The pricing curve rewards early voters. If you're confident a country will qualify — say, Brazil, England, or another nation with a huge global fanbase — vote early. You get more votes for less ETH, which means a larger share of the prize pool if they qualify.

The risk: popular countries attract a lot of votes, which dilutes your share.

**Strategy 2: Find the undervalued fanbases**

A country doesn't need to be a football powerhouse to qualify — it needs a passionate, onchain-native fanbase that mobilises quickly.

Think about which countries are overrepresented in crypto communities. Which nations have large, coordinated diaspora communities online? A country with fewer total fans but a highly engaged crypto community could outperform expectations.

Early votes on these countries are cheap. The upside if they qualify is significant.

**Strategy 3: Spread across multiple countries**

You can vote for as many countries as you want. Spreading votes across several likely qualifiers reduces your exposure to any single country failing to make it.

The trade-off: spreading dilutes your share in any individual country's prize pool. Concentration is higher risk, higher reward.

**Strategy 4: Watch the leaderboard and react**

The leaderboard updates in real time. If a country you voted for is sitting safely in the top 48, you may not need to add more votes. If a country you care about is on the bubble, adding votes in the final hours could be the difference.

But remember: prices go up as votes accumulate. Late votes are expensive.

**The maths in plain terms**

Your payout = (your votes for qualifying countries ÷ total votes across all qualifying countries) × 90% of total prize pool.

More votes + cheaper entry (earlier voting) + qualifying country = maximum return.
