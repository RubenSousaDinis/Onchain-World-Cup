## Account Strategy

### App Accounts (Official)
These are the branded channels. Content is polished, focused on the product, and speaks to the audience as a whole.

- **Farcaster**: @onchainworldcup (or equivalent)
- **Twitter/X**: @OnchainWorldCup (or equivalent)
- **Tone**: Direct, exciting, community-first

### Builder Accounts (Personal)
These are the builder's personal accounts. They post **in addition to** the app accounts. Personal posts carry trust signals that branded accounts cannot — they show real people are behind the project. Builder accounts should post candidly about the build, their perspective, and behind-the-scenes context.

- **Who posts**: Lead builder(s) from their personal handles
- **Tone**: First-person, candid, "build in public"
- **Do not**: Cross-post identical content. Each account adds something different.
- **Do**: Tag the app account and link to the app in personal posts

## Content Pillars

All AI-generated content must stay within one of these five pillars:

1. **Build in Public** — Behind-the-scenes, dev progress, contract updates
2. **Mechanics Explained** — How pricing works, how prize pool splits, how qualification works
3. **Prize Pool Hype** — Pool size milestones, growth trajectory, urgency
4. **Country Pride** — National rivalries, leaderboard updates, fan callouts
5. **Countdown / Urgency** — Days remaining, final hours, last-chance messaging

---

## Branding Rules for AI

- Always write "Onchain World Cup" — never "Crypto World Cup"
- ETH prize pool must always be shown net of fees — never the gross total
- Qualification has no matches, only votes
- Top 48 countries qualify (not top 32, not top 64)
- Votes cost ETH, price increases as more votes are cast for a country
- Users who backed qualified countries share the prize pool proportionally

## AI Content Generation Instructions

When generating content for this project, follow these rules:

### Voice and Tone
- Short. Direct. No marketing fluff.
- No emojis unless listing countries (flag emojis are fine) or using 🏆 sparingly
- Never say "exciting opportunity" or "revolutionary"
- Numbers first: lead with data (ETH, votes, days, rankings)
- Trust is earned through transparency, not enthusiasm

### What to Always Include
- App link in every post
- Specific numbers when available (prize pool, votes, days remaining)
- The qualification close date (end of May) when urgency is relevant
- The main tournament start date (June 11) when building anticipation
- Clarification that only top 48 qualify

### What to Never Write
- "Crypto World Cup" — always "Onchain World Cup"
- Prize pool as gross amount — always net of fees
- Anything implying guaranteed returns
- Anything claiming this is investment advice

### Per-Channel Formatting

**Farcaster:**
- Max ~320 characters for the main cast
- Use frames/embeds where possible
- Tag relevant channels: /worldcup /base /onchain
- Reply to community posts in those channels

**Twitter/X:**
- Keep threads to 3–5 tweets max
- Lead tweet must hook without the thread
- Engagement bait is fine: "Which country are you backing?"

**Builder (personal) accounts:**
- First-person always
- More candid, behind-the-scenes tone
- Can express uncertainty, challenges, excitement — things a brand account wouldn't
- Tag the app account (@onchainworldcup or equivalent) but don't just repeat its posts
- Aim for 2–4 sentences. Less is more on personal accounts.

## Builder Account Content Principles

The builder's personal accounts are one of the most powerful distribution channels. Here's how to use them effectively:

**Do:**
- Post raw thoughts about the build, decisions made, and why
- Share observations about voter behavior once qualification starts
- Respond directly to people asking about the project
- Post during launch with genuine real-time reactions
- Use first-person: "I built this because...", "We decided to..."

**Don't:**
- Copy-paste from app account posts
- Sound like a press release
- Post the same content twice across personal and app accounts
- Over-promote — one mention per cluster of personal posts is enough

**Example cadence during qualification:**
- Launch day: 1 personal post reacting to going live
- Every 2–3 days: observation or reflection on what's happening
- Final 48 hours: 1–2 genuine urgency posts
